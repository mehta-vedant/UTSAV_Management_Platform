01. AML Fundamentals

What AML Is

Anti-Money Laundering (AML) = laws, regulations, controls and procedures that financial institutions use to prevent, detect and report money laundering, terrorist financing (TF) and proliferation financing (PF). Usually written AML/CFT/CPF.

Money laundering = disguising the proceeds of crime as legitimate funds.

The 3 Stages of Money Laundering MEMORIZE

StageWhat happensDetection hook
PlacementGet illicit cash into the financial system: cash deposits split below reporting thresholds ("structuring"), cash-intensive business deposits, money instruments, forex, gambling chips.Threshold rules, structuring detection, cash-to-account ratios
LayeringMove funds to obscure origin/ownership: rapid wires across accounts/banks/countries, shell companies, trade transactions, crypto, asset purchases.Velocity/wire rules, pass-through detection, high-risk jurisdiction rules
IntegrationRe-introduce funds as apparently legitimate wealth: real estate, luxury goods, shares, self-loans from shell companies.Net-worth vs income anomalies, credit repayment source checks

Why Banks Must Do AML

  • Legal obligation — failing to file STRs is a crime in many jurisdictions.
  • Regulatory fines & license actions; reputational damage; de-risking by correspondent banks; criminal liability for individuals.
  • 2024–2026 enforcement shifted from "no system" to ineffective systems — stale rules, poor tuning, false-positive floods masking real activity.

Regulatory Framework (Global)

  • FATF (Financial Action Task Force): 40 Recommendations = global standard; risk-based approach (RBA) guidance; grey/black lists of high-risk jurisdictions; 2025 updates to R16 Travel Rule and R15 (virtual assets).
  • Basel Committee (BCBS): "Sound management of risks related to ML/TF"; Pillar 2 expectation.
  • Wolfsberg Group: industry guidance — correspondent banking, trade finance, sanctions, PEPs.
  • Egmont Group: network of Financial Intelligence Units (FIUs).
  • UN Security Council resolutions (1267/1989 Al-Qaida/ISIL, 1373 TF) — legally binding.

KYC / CDD / EDD / pKYC

  • KYC (Know Your Customer) — umbrella: identify, verify, understand purpose & nature of relationship.
  • CDD (Customer Due Diligence) — baseline at onboarding & trigger events: full name, DOB, address, ID verification (passport / Emirates ID / NRIC), beneficial ownership, source of funds/wealth. For entities: directors, signatories, UBOs (≥25% ownership/control typically), corporate registry, proof of structure.
  • EDD (Enhanced Due Diligence) — triggered by PEP status, high-risk jurisdiction, unusual activity, complex structures: source-of-wealth substantiation, senior management/MLRO approval, enhanced monitoring, tighter thresholds.
  • pKYC (perpetual KYC) — continuous, event-driven refresh instead of fixed annual cycles.

Sanctions & Screening Lists

  • Key lists: UN Consolidated, OFAC SDN (incl. the 50% rule — an entity owned ≥50% by a listed person is blocked even if unlisted), EU Consolidated, UK OFSI, plus local lists (UAE Local Terrorist List; HK UN Sanctions Ordinance).
  • Match types: true match vs false positive (matched but innocent — cost) vs false negative (missed — the dangerous one).
  • PEP = individuals entrusted with prominent public functions (heads of state, ministers, judges, military, central-bank officials, SOE heads) + family members & close associates. PEP → EDD + senior approval + source-of-funds/wealth checks + typically a 12-month cooling-off period after leaving office.
  • Adverse media = negative news (corruption, fraud, sanctions, ML, crime). Must distinguish direct vs indirect mentions; feeds risk scoring and EDD.
Answer framing: "Screening must cover name, DOB, nationality, jurisdiction, ID/passport numbers. The consequence of a missed sanctions hit is severe, so sanctions lists use conservative/lower match thresholds, accepting more false positives; PEP lists can tolerate fewer."

02. Transaction Monitoring (TM)

Purpose & Filing

Detect transactions inconsistent with a customer's profile or known typologies; generate alerts; investigate; file STR (Suspicious Transaction Report — FATF/APAC/ME/EU) or SAR (Suspicious Activity Report — US/FinCEN). Deadlines vary by jurisdiction (same-day to ~15 days).

Common Typologies & Behaviors KNOW 5+

  • Structuring / smurfing — deposits just below threshold (e.g., $9,000–9,900 under $10k) or many small deposits by several people into one account.
  • Threshold breach — cash/reporting threshold or customer-specific thresholds (e.g., >$50k/month vs historical average).
  • Rapid in-and-out / pass-through ("churn") — funds deposited then immediately moved; low retained balance.
  • Layering via wires — many cross-border wires, high-risk jurisdictions, multiple beneficiaries, round-trips.
  • Unexpected activity — activity inconsistent with declared profile (teacher receiving $100k wires).
  • High-risk counterparties — shell companies, MSBs/remittance agents, casinos, crypto exchanges, offshore havens.
  • Trade-based ML — over/under-invoicing, phantom shipments, valuation mismatches.
  • Cash-intensive businesses — cash deposits inconsistent with turnover.
  • Loan repaid by unverified sources; multiple related accounts (link analysis); jewelry/real estate with opaque funding.

Rules Engine & Tuning

  • Scenario-based rules: e.g., "Cash deposits >30% of declared monthly income in 7 days", "3+ wires >$25k in 30 days", "monthly turnover >5x 90-day average".
  • Implemented as SQL/SAS aggregations: daily/monthly totals, moving averages, velocity counts, joined to customer risk ratings, compared to rule parameters.
  • Segment-based thresholds (retail / SME / corporate / HNW / MSB) prevent uniform thresholds from flooding alerts.
  • False positives drive tuning: adjust thresholds, add exclusions/exceptions, calibrate per segment. Track every change in a tuning log for regulator evidence.

Alert → Case Workflow MEMORIZE

  1. Alert generation — rule/ML fires; alert carries customer, rule, trigger details, txn references, risk score.
  2. Alert triage/queuing — routed by severity/segment; SLA clock starts.
  3. Investigation — analyst reviews profile, transaction history, screening hits, adverse media, link analysis; writes narrative.
  4. Disposition — False positive (close) or True positive (escalate).
  5. Case creation — related alerts consolidated into a case.
  6. STR/SAR filing — MLRO/Compliance approves; filed via GoAML / STREAMS; tipping-off prohibited (do not alert the subject).
  7. Closure & audit trail — documented rationale; records kept ≥5–6 years; MIS metrics feed governance.

Key Metrics BE INTERVIEW-READY

False Positive Rate

FP / total alerts. Industry historically 90–95%+; modern systems push precision much higher.

Alert-to-SAR conversion

~5–15% benchmark (many sit at 2–5%). Low conversion = too many alerts; high = missed detection risk.

Time to disposition

Aging of cases, review queue depth, SLA attainment.

SAR quality

Regulators penalize thin narratives — specific dates, amounts, counterparties, deviation from profile.
How would you detect structuring at 100M+ transactions?
Aggregate cash transactions per customer/account per day/week; flag multiples just below threshold (e.g., $9k–9.9k); link accounts by shared owner/UBO/beneficiary; cluster by counterparty; implement as SQL aggregations + rules, tuned per segment.
How do you compute a customer's transaction baseline?
Rolling 30/90-day averages, percentiles, moving median (robust to outliers); compare current velocity vs baseline; recompute nightly in batch.
What makes a high-quality STR?
Clear narrative of why the activity is suspicious, specific dates/amounts/counterparties, deviation from profile, links to other activity, supporting documents.

03. Watchlist / Screening System Design

Exact vs Fuzzy Matching

  • Exact — normalized string equality: low recall (misses Mohammed vs Mohammad), low false positives.
  • Fuzzy — similarity scoring: higher recall, higher false positives; tunable thresholds.

Name Normalization Pipeline CODE-READY

  • Strip diacritics (Muhammad/Mohammed), transliterate to Latin script (Arabic, Chinese pinyin).
  • Uppercase, trim, collapse whitespace, remove punctuation.
  • Tokenize first/middle/last; handle name-order cultures, titles (Sheikh, Haji), honorifics.
  • Handle aliases, name variants (Mohammad/Mohammed/Mohamed/Muhammad), initials, reversed order.
  • Phonetic keys: Soundex, Double Metaphone, NYSIIS (so "Smyth" ~ "Smith").
  • Edit distance: Levenshtein (insert/delete/substitute; distance ≤1–2 for typos), Damerau-Levenshtein (+transpositions), Jaro-Winkler (prefix weighting, common for names), bigram/trigram similarity.
  • Token-set comparison + permutation of middle names; blocking (e.g., soundex + DOB) to cut the candidate set before scoring at 40M scale.

Match Scoring & Disposition

  • Composite score (0–100) blending phonetic + edit distance + token + exact hits; weights per field (name > DOB > ID > nationality > address).
  • Field corroboration: DOB/ID match overrides a weak name match (raises confidence); name-only match → analyst review.
  • Three-tier: auto-match (≥ threshold) / possible (review) / no match (clear). Sanctions lists use lower thresholds (conservative); PEP tolerates more FPs.
  • Entity resolution / dedup — collapse customer records into one identity (name+DOB+ID+address+phone); essential for correct link analysis and avoiding duplicate STRs.

Batch vs Real-Time

BatchReal-time
UsePortfolio back-screening, list updates, transaction sweeps, adverse-media ingestionOnboarding, payment time, ISO 20022 messages, instant payments
LatencyDaily / near-real-timeSub-second (p95 < 500ms–1s)
StackADF pipelines, SAS jobs, scheduled runsIn-memory watchlist caches, low-latency match service, pre-computed n-gram indexes
  • List lifecycle: high-frequency list updates (UN/OFAC SDN/EU/local), delta processing, back-screening existing customers on list changes ("look-back"), audit of list version per screen.
Explain fuzzy name matching in screening.
Normalization (diacritics, transliteration, tokenization) → phonetic keys (Soundex/Double Metaphone) → edit distance (Levenshtein/Damerau) + Jaro-Winkler → scoring with field corroboration (DOB/ID/nationality) → threshold triage (auto/possible/clear).
How do you reconcile 40M customers nightly against updated watchlists?
Back-screening job: new-list delta → fuzzy match against customer master (with blocking keys) → generate hits → queue to analysts. Track list version + screening coverage metrics; audit list version used per screen.
How would you reduce false positives?
Tune thresholds per segment, add corroborating fields (DOB/ID/nationality), suppress same-list repeat hits, allowlist after review, ML re-ranking, and measure FPR continuously with a tuning log.

Typical Endpoints

  • POST /api/v1/screenings — submit screening/risk-assessment request (sync or async).
  • GET /api/v1/screenings/{screeningId} — poll status/result.

Authentication & Security

  • API keys — X-API-Key header; scoped, rotated, stored in Azure Key Vault.
  • OAuth2 client-credentials — POST /token with client_id/client_secret → bearer token with scopes (screening:read, screening:write); TTL + refresh. Most common for enterprise banking.
  • mTLS — both sides present certificates; common for inter-bank/regulator integrations; certificate lifecycle management.
  • HMAC signing for integrity/non-repudiation.

Robustness Engineering LIKELY PANEL FOCUS

  • Rate limiting: headers X-RateLimit-Limit/Remaining/Reset; HTTP 429 + Retry-After; client exponential backoff + jitter (1s, 2s, 4s...).
  • Retries: only for idempotent ops / safe failures (5xx, timeout, 429); never blindly retry 4xx (400/401/403/422 are permanent).
  • Idempotency: client-supplied key; server dedupes & returns cached result on retry (no duplicate screenings).
  • Timeouts: connect (~2–3s), read, total; short-circuit sync path and fall back to async.
  • Circuit breaker: trip after N consecutive failures (e.g., 5 in 10s), open for cool-down, half-open probe — prevents cascading load on a degraded service.
  • Sync vs async: sync for low-latency single-entity screening (p95 <1–2s); async + webhook/polling for batch & heavy list matching. At 40M scale prefer async.
  • Error contract: { "error": { "code": "INVALID_SUBJECT", "message": "...", "field": "dateOfBirth", "requestId": "..." } } mapped to HTTP statuses (400/401/403/404/422/429/5xx).
  • Logging & audit: structured JSON logs with requestId/traceId correlation; audit logs record who, what list version, scores, dispositions (regulatory evidence). Never log raw PII/keys.
How do you make a screening API idempotent?
Client supplies an idempotency key (the screeningId). Server stores key→result; a retry returns the cached response instead of creating a duplicate screening.
How do you handle a screening vendor that times out?
Set timeouts; retry with backoff only on 5xx/timeouts/429; add a circuit breaker; fall back to async batch; alert on thresholds.

05. Risk Scoring & Typical Data Model

Risk-Based Approach (RBA)

FATF core principle: allocate controls proportional to assessed risk; regulators expect a documented risk assessment, not blanket measures.

Risk Factors

  • Customer: PEP, sanctions-listed, adverse media, high-risk occupation, MSB/crypto exposure, complex ownership, cash-intensive.
  • Geographic: FATF grey/black list, corruption index, offshore centers, sanctions exposure.
  • Product: private banking, correspondent, trade finance, crypto, prepaid cards, cross-border wires.
  • Channel: remote onboarding, digital-only, third-party introducers, cash-heavy channels.
  • Behavior: velocity, volume, unusual counterparties, deviations from expected profile.

Scoring Model

06. Credit Risk Fundamentals

What It Is & Types

Credit risk = borrower/counterparty fails to meet obligations, causing loss. The largest risk category in most banks' balance sheets.

  • Retail — personal loans, cards, mortgages, auto, microfinance: high volume, low value, pooled treatment.
  • Corporate/wholesale — loans, trade/project finance, syndications: low volume, high value, individually assessed.
  • Counterparty (CCR) — derivatives/trading counterparties; measured under SA-CCR.
  • Concentration — undiversified exposure; large-exposure limits (e.g., ≤25% Tier 1 to one counterparty under Basel).
  • Country/sovereign/transfer — FX controls, war, sanctions prevent repayment.
  • Settlement — one party performs before receiving value (Herstatt risk).

Credit Lifecycle

Origination → Underwriting/Decisioning → Monitoring → Collections & Recovery → Write-off (charged-off ~180 DPD retail; then recoveries).

PD / LGD / EAD / EL MEMORIZE FORMULAS

  • PD — Probability of Default within 1 year (or remaining life); from scorecards/ratings.
  • LGD — Loss Given Default = 1 − Recovery Rate. Typical: 5–10% mortgages, 40–60% unsecured personal, 75–90% cards.
  • EAD — Exposure at Default = drawn + CCF × undrawn (cards ~75–100% CCF on unused limit).
  • EL = PD × LGD × EAD. EL is covered by provisions/pricing; UL (unexpected loss) is covered by regulatory capital.

Basel I / II / III

  • Basel I (1988): flat risk weights, 8% minimum capital.
  • Basel II (2004): 3 Pillars — (1) minimum capital for credit/market/operational risk, (2) supervisory review (ICAAP), (3) market discipline. Introduced Standardised Approach (SA) and IRB (Foundation/Advanced, internal PD/LGD/EAD).
  • Basel III / 3.1 "Endgame": CET1 4.5% + buffers (~10.5% incl. CCB); leverage ratio; LCR/NSFR; SA-CCR; output floor capped at 72.5% of standardised RWA (phased to 2028); revised standardised weights; minimum LGD floors. Timelines: UK 2027, EU/JP ~2025–28, US re-proposed 2026, ME/Asia aligning 2027–28.

RWA = Exposure × Risk Weight. Capital ratio = Tier 1/2 ÷ RWA. Consumer loan ~75% RW (SA); corporate 20–150% by rating; sovereign 0–150%.

IFRS 9 (ECL) CRITICAL

StageConditionProvision
Stage 1Performing; no significant increase in credit risk (SICR)12-month ECL

  • SICR thresholds are the crux: relative PD increase (e.g., ≥300%) and/or absolute (≥30 DPD).
  • ECL = Σ PD(t) × LGD(t) × EAD(t) × DF(t) over 12M (S1) or lifetime (S2/S3), weighted across macro scenarios (e.g., 60/20/20 base/upside/downside).
  • PiT vs TtC PDs: IFRS 9 uses forward-looking PiT; Basel uses long-run TtC. US equivalent = CECL. India (RBI) moving to ECL from 1 Apr 2027. GCC banks follow IFRS 9 under SAMA/DFSA/CBUAE.
  • Post-Model Adjustments (PMAs)/overlays for risks models can't capture — a big audit/regulator focus (BoE SS1/23).
Explain PD, LGD, EAD for a credit-card portfolio.
PD from behavioural scorecard/bureau (90+ DPD within 12M), estimated per score band; LGD = 1 − recovery (~75–90% unsecured); EAD = outstanding + CCF×undrawn limit. EL = PD×LGD×EAD; multiply per band and sum.
What is the Basel III output floor?
Capital from internal (IRB) models cannot be below 72.5% of standardised RWA — prevents model under-estimation; phased in to 2028; raises RWA for many banks.

07. Credit Scoring & Retail Portfolios

Scorecards

  • Map borrower attributes → score ranking P(good/bad); "bad" typically = 90+ DPD / charge-off within 12–24 months.
  • Logistic regression is the workhorse: logit(P(bad)) = β0 + β1x1 + ...; coefficients scaled to score points.
  • WoE (weight of evidence) + IV (information value) for variable selection (IV>0.3 strong, 0.1–0.3 moderate, <0.02 weak). Gini/KS/AR measure discrimination (retail card Gini ~0.55–0.65 good).
  • Application scoring (at origination, static) vs behavioural scoring (ongoing account behavior, more predictive — powers limit management and EWS).
  • Cut-off drives accept/reject and pricing; odds = (1−P_bad)/P_bad; score bands feed pricing grids, limits, and IFRS 9 PD term structures.

Typical Retail Features

Income/stability, DTI (≤40–50% typical), LTV (80% vs 90% materially changes PD/LGD), utilization (>60–70% is a strong risk signal), payment history, bureau enquiries, delinquency, length of credit history, product mix, plus cash-flow/alternative data.

What are roll rates and why do they matter?
% of accounts in bucket X moving to X+30 next month. They forecast NPL flow, size collections capacity, and validate EWS timing.
Application vs behavioural scoring?
Application = static, at origination (income, bureau); behavioural = dynamic account usage (utilisation, DPD), more predictive; powers limit management and EWS.

08. Early Warning Systems (EWS)

Purpose

Detect credit-quality deterioration before default so the bank can act early. Converts periodic/annual review into continuous, trigger-driven surveillance. Also feeds IFRS 9 Stage 2 (SICR) detection.

Internal vs External Signals

  • Internal: missed/partial payments, DPD trends, utilisation spikes, payment-pattern change, exposure changes, covenant breaches, limit drawdowns, falling deposits/balances, cash advances, returned EMIs/cheques.
  • External: bureau score drops, new high-cost enquiries, negative news/sentiment, rating downgrades, industry stress, regulatory action, litigation, sanctions/PEP flags, macro/interest-rate environment.

Key Early Warning Indicators (EWIs) SAY 5+

  • First 30 DPD, DPD trend (0→15→30), missed/partial payments.
  • Utilisation spike (>90% for 2+ months); frequent cash advances; returned EMIs/cheques; unauthorised overdraft.
  • Bureau score drop (>50 points in 6 months) or new delinquencies elsewhere.
  • Corporate: covenant breaches (DSCR <1.2x, leverage > limit), overdraft, delays in financials, key-man events.
  • Limit/credit-line increases right after deterioration; drawdowns on committed facilities.
  • Reduced salary credits, balance run-off, disappearing deposits (cash-flow stress).
  • Industry/sector stress (oil price, real-estate cycle), geographic stress, currency risk.
  • Negative news, sanctions/litigation, guarantor/counterparty deterioration, collateral LTV breach.

EWS Workflow MEMORIZE

  1. Data ingestion — batch (daily/monthly) + real-time REST events from core banking (Oracle Flexcube, Finacle, Temenos), bureau, news.
  2. Trigger evaluation — rule engine scores each EWI vs thresholds (e.g., "≥2 triggers" or score ≥ X).
  3. Scoring/alert — EWS risk score (0–100 or Low/Med/High); blends internal behavioural score + external factors.
  4. Case creation — watchlist entry in the risk workbench/CRM.
  5. Review by risk officer — validate alerts (some are false positives: e.g., seasonal utilisation); confirm rating.
  6. Remediation — increase review frequency, reduce/block limit, watchlist, restructure, move to collections, additional collateral/guarantees, or move to Stage 2.

Cure Rate, Watch List, Risk Migration

  • Cure rate = % of defaulted/Stage-2 accounts returning to performing within a window (e.g., 6 months). High cure rate → EWS alerting too early/broadly; used in LGD and staging decisions; redefault risk matters.
  • Watch list = accounts with elevated risk, graded (e.g., 1–4) with mandatory review frequency (quarterly → weekly). EWS feeds watchlist; watchlist feeds "flow into NPL".
  • Risk migration = rating/score movement over time (migration/transition matrices, Markov-style); EWS models predict probability of migration to a worse bucket.
How would you build an EWS for 40M customers?
Score triggers at customer+account level; rules in a decision engine fed by daily batch + real-time API events; tiered alerts; case management; feedback loop to tune thresholds (hit-rate vs false positives). Start with a risk mart in Oracle/PG, ADF for orchestration, SAS for scoring.
Your EWS is raising too many false positives. What do you do?
Analyse alert-to-default hit rate, tune threshold weights, add confirmation triggers (2+ signals), use cure rate to calibrate, and segment thresholds by product.
Watchlist vs Stage 2 account?
Watchlist is internal risk-management classification; Stage 2 is IFRS 9 accounting driven by SICR. Overlapping but not identical — watchlist is usually broader/forward-looking.

10. ETL / ELT & Data Modeling

ETL vs ELT

  • ETL — transform before load; suits mature relational DWs, strong validation before commit, transformation-heavy.
  • ELT — load raw into a scalable target (lake/lakehouse/Snowflake/Synapse) then transform in place; schema-on-read, massive data, batch + near-real-time.

Banking nuance: regulatory reporting often mandates ETL-style validation before the warehouse accepts data — reject/flag early rather than polluting the warehouse.

Full vs Incremental vs Streaming

  • Full load: truncate + reload. Simple; only for small reference tables (branch, FX rates) or initial backfill.
  • Incremental/delta: watermark/high-water mark (control table tracks last_load_dt/max PK) — WHERE last_updated > :watermark; fragile if timestamps drift/backdated.
  • CDC (change data capture): log-based (best — Oracle GoldenGate, Debezium; captures deletes + before-images, zero source impact), trigger-based (source overhead), or table-compare (expensive at 100M).
  • Streaming: Kafka → Debezium → landing; for real-time fraud/balances; combined with EOD batch reconciliation.

DW vs Lake vs Lakehouse

  • DW: structured, curated, governed, fast BI (Oracle, Synapse, Teradata); expensive.
  • Lake: raw any-format, cheap; risk of "data swamp".
  • Lakehouse: ACID on object storage via Delta Lake / Iceberg / Hudi; medallion architecture: Bronze (raw) → Silver (cleaned/deduped) → Gold (curated marts).

Schemas & Dimensional Modeling CORE

  • Star — one fact + denormalized dims; fast, Kimball marts.
  • Snowflake — normalized sub-dimensions; less redundancy, more joins.
  • 3NF — fully normalized enterprise model (Inmon / OLTP).
  • Facts: additive (amounts), semi-additive (balances — not summable over time), non-additive (ratios). Types: transaction, periodic snapshot (daily balance), accumulating snapshot (loan lifecycle).
  • Dimensions: conformed (same key everywhere — critical in banking), degenerate (e.g., txn reference), junk, role-playing (Date as Open/Close/Value date).
  • Surrogate keys — meaningless integer PK, independent of source business keys (insulates against source merges).
  • SCD1 overwrite (no history); SCD2 new row + effective_from/to/is_current (full history — used for addresses, KYC, limits); SCD3 current + one prior value.
  • Kimball (bottom-up marts, conformed dims, faster) vs Inmon (top-down 3NF EDW first, then marts; more enterprise consistency, slower).

End-to-End Scenario: "Design ETL to load 40M customers + 100M+ txns daily" WALK THROUGH

  1. Sources: on-prem Oracle core + PG side systems. Extract: log-based CDC for transactions; watermark for customer dim.
  2. Landing: immutable raw zone with src/db/table/biz_dt/; checksums + row counts.
  3. Staging: mirrored schema + validation; rejected rows held with reason codes.
  4. Transform: ADF orchestration + SAS/SQL (or Databricks); standardize codes/dates; dedup; surrogate-key lookup.
  5. Dim load (SCD2): compare incoming with current; insert new version on change, flip is_current.
  6. Fact load: MERGE/insert into monthly txn_fact partitions; local indexes; ANALYZE after load.
  7. Marts: daily balances, aggregations.
  8. Scheduling: Control-M/Airflow top-level DAG; ADF triggers per stage; business-day calendar; parameterized rerun by biz_dt.
  9. Reconciliation: row counts (extract vs landing vs staging vs target per partition), SUM/count control totals, balance check vs GL, hash compare, DQ gates that block load.
  10. Monitoring: failure/zero-row/reconciliation-breach alerts, SLA timers, SHIR health, daily EOD report.
  11. Failure handling: retry (transient) → quarantine → checkpoint resume → on-call escalation → post-mortem.

Indexing, Execution Plans, Tuning

  • Indexes: B-tree default; composite (leading col must be used); unique (idempotency); covering/INCLUDE; partial (PG WHERE status='ACTIVE'); function-based (Oracle ON UPPER(name)).
  • Plans: Seq vs Index scan; Nested Loop (small outer × indexed inner), Hash Join (large unsorted equi-joins — best at 100M), Merge Join (sorted). Look for seq scans on big tables, spills, stale stats.
  • Tuning: index join/filter cols; partition pruning; ANALYZE/GATHER_TABLE_STATS after loads; avoid WHERE func(col)=x; set-based over row-by-row; pre-aggregate; avoid SELECT *.
  • Batch: Oracle FORALL/bulk collect; PG batched INSERT; chunked commits (e.g., 10k rows); parallel streams per partition.
  • Views = virtual convenience/access control; materialized views = precomputed physical aggregates for performance on huge data.

SQL Interview Q&A

ROW_NUMBER vs RANK vs DENSE_RANK?
RN = unique row numbers; RANK has gaps on ties; DENSE_RANK no gaps. Use RANK when ties should share rank.
How to find duplicate customers?
GROUP BY nat_key HAVING COUNT(*)>1, or ROW_NUMBER() PARTITION BY nat_key.
NOT IN vs NOT EXISTS?
NOT IN fails if the subquery contains NULLs (three-valued logic); NOT EXISTS is semantically safe and usually faster.
How do you tune a 100M-row join?
Analyze stats; hash join; partition both sides on the same key (partition-wise join); covering indexes; filter before join; pre-aggregate; parallel query.
What is partition pruning?
The optimizer skips irrelevant partitions via WHERE txn_dt BETWEEN ... on a range-partitioned table — a huge scan becomes tiny.

15. Scale: 40M Customers / 100M+ Transactions

Partitioning

  • Facts by range on date (monthly partitions; Oracle interval auto-creates) → partition pruning; roll-offs = drop partition (fast).
  • Hash by key (cust_id) for even distribution + parallel scans.
  • Composite — range + hash subpartitions for hot months; local indexes per partition for cheap maintenance.

Indexing, Parallelism, Incremental, Idempotency

  • Composite covering indexes; partial indexes; function-based for MDM; ANALYZE regularly.
  • Oracle /*+ PARALLEL(t,8) */; PG max_parallel_workers_per_gather; ADF parallel DIUs; chunk reads by PK/date ranges.
  • Watermark/high-water-mark per table + log-based CDC; never full-refresh 100M rows daily.
  • Staging → atomic MERGE/upsert by unique natural key; load_job_id; deterministic ORDER BY; TRUNCATE+INSERT per partition; transactional boundaries.
  • Checkpointing: track last completed chunk in a control table; resume from checkpoint, don't restart the whole load.
  • Skew: salting hot keys (append 0–N), broadcast small dims, repartition data flows; avoid one hot partition on inserts.
  • Archival: drop old partitions to archive schemas (legal retention ~7 years — archive, don't destroy), partition exchange to swap cold data out instantly.
  • Joins: hash joins, partition-aligned joins, pre-filter/pre-aggregate, surrogate keys (int joins cheap), pre-aggregated marts so reports never scan 100M rows.

16. Multi-Source Integration & MDM

  • Heterogeneity: core banking (Oracle/DB2/mainframe), payments switches (ISO8583), cards, CRM, channels, files (CSV/fixed-width/EBCDIC), REST APIs, queues (Kafka/MQ). Problems: different keys, formats, encodings, code lists, currencies, time zones, semantics.
  • Landing: raw files/unloads preserved (immutable) with source, load_dt, batch_id; log file name, checksum, row count, received_at, status.
  • Staging: mirrored schemas, lightly standardized; holds rejected rows; enables reprocessing without re-hitting sources.
  • Standardization: normalize codes (AE→UAE→784), ISO 4217 currency, common date/timezone (UTC + business calendar), names/addresses.
  • MDM / customer master: exact match on national ID/email; fuzzy/score-based on name+DOB+address; survivorship/golden record (most complete/trusted per field); customer_master + customer_map (src_sys, src_cust_id, cust_master_id); daily match/merge jobs; audit merges (merging the wrong customers is a serious banking risk).
  • Lineage: track source → landing → staging → master → marts (source_system, table, extraction_time, job_id, transform rules).
  • Error handling: quarantine/rejection tables with reason + raw payload; retry with backoff; zero-downtime pattern (load to staging, validate, promote via rename/partition-swap); alert on error thresholds; fail the job on material discrepancy.

18. Production Issue Resolution

Triage

  1. Scope: single job/feed or systemic? Source vs target vs transform?
  2. Check monitor/alerts → logs → data. Stabilize (hold dependent jobs) before deep-diving.
  3. Communicate status to stakeholders early.

RCA & Classification

  • 5 Whys / fishbone; classify: data issue (backdated txn, schema change → fix source/re-extract), code issue (fix + rerun), infra issue (network/disk/DB locks/SHIR down → rerun after recovery), dependency, parameter/calendar.
  • Evidence: job logs, run history, source timestamps, row-level diffs.

Monitoring & Scheduling Tools

  • Alert on: failure, late start (SLA), zero rows loaded, reconciliation breach, SHIR heartbeat down.
  • Control-M (enterprise batch: dependencies, business-day calendars, SLA alerts — common in banks), Apache Airflow (DAGs, retries, backfills, sensors), ADF triggers.
  • Design: dependency graph, task-level reruns, holiday calendars, resource contention handling.

Rerun / Recovery / Incident / Post-mortem

  • Idempotent reruns (staging + upsert + watermark); checkpoint resume from failed partition; partial rerun; backfill via parameterized run (biz_dt); rollback from staging/pre-load backup; document data-versioning so consumers know what changed.
  • Severities: Sev1 = EOD batch down/financial misstatement; Sev2 = single feed delayed; Sev3 = cosmetic. SLA response targets, owner/on-call, escalation path (ITSM/ServiceNow).
  • Post-mortem: timeline, impact, root cause, containment, prevention (tests, validation rules, monitoring, runbooks), follow-up actions with owners/dates — blameless culture.
A production job failed at 3am, what do you do?
(1) Assess impact — deadline today? who must know? (2) Open incident, pull the rota. (3) Diagnose from logs: data, code, or infra. (4) If data → correct and rerun after smoke test; if code → approved fix path, never improvise in prod. (5) Verify outputs + reconcile totals vs prior run. (6) Communicate upward. (7) Drive RCA: root cause, why not caught earlier, the control to prevent recurrence. "Slow is smooth, smooth is fast" — controlled steps beat panic reruns.

19. SDLC & Consulting in Banking

Waterfall vs Agile vs Hybrid

  • Waterfall: sequential, phase-gated, signed off — favored for regulatory/risk/core-banking implementations (auditability).
  • Agile: sprints, backlog, working software each sprint — front-office channels, BI, risk analytics tools.

20. Requirement Analysis

Gathering Techniques

Interviews (exceptions are where requirements hide), workshops, JAD sessions, document analysis (existing reports, regulator circulars, data dictionary), shadowing, prototyping/mockups, questionnaires.

BRD vs FRD vs SRS

  • BRD — what the business wants and why (objectives, scope, stakeholders, success criteria) — for sponsors.
  • FRD — how the system must behave (functions, rules, validations, screen/report logic) — for developers/QA.
  • SRS — consolidated formal spec (functional + non-functional + interface + constraints), contract-level in vendor projects. In many banks FRD ≈ SRS.

Functional vs Non-Functional (NFRs)

  • Functional: "the report must show ECL by portfolio and product", "the API must validate customer ID".
  • Non-functional: performance (batch before 06:00, report <5s), security (encryption, RBAC, PII masking), availability/DR (RTO/RPO), scalability, compliance/auditability (lineage, immutable audit trail), usability.
Interview tip: regulators/architects probe NFRs harder than FRs. Always ask "what happens at month-end with 10x data?"

MoSCoW, User Stories, As-Is/To-Be, Traceability

  • MoSCoW: Must / Should / Could / Won't — "Won't have" is as valuable as Must; use it when a deadline slips instead of silently cutting.
  • User stories + acceptance criteria: Given a corporate customer with a past-due installment, when the EWS batch runs, then a watchlist alert is generated with reason code PAST_DUE_30.
  • As-is vs to-be: document current (often Excel-driven) flow; gap analysis; to-be removes the manual layer.
  • Traceability matrix: every requirement → design → test case → evidence (FR-014 → DM-09 → UAT TC-57 → Passed 12-Mar) — proves to audit everything was built & tested.
  • Sign-off: right people (authorized business owners) sign after walkthrough; route regulatory requirements through compliance/IT risk.

Scope Creep & Change Requests

Counter with a baselined, signed scope + change control: any new request → CR with impact analysis → CCB approval → update BRD/mapping/tests. Realistic scenario: two weeks before UAT the client adds a regulator field → "Impact: +6 dev days, +3 UAT days, go-live moves to 14-Sep, or we ship without it." Let the business decide.

21. UAT Support

What UAT Is

Business users (not developers) test that the system meets business requirements in a production-like environment — "fit for purpose", not "does the code work".

UAT Plan & Test Data

  • Plan: scope, entry/exit criteria, environment, testers, data strategy, schedule, defect process, sign-off criteria.
  • Scenarios: end-to-end business journeys + negative tests + regulatory scenarios (loan goes 90-dpd → check EWS alert + provisioning output). Map each to a requirement.
  • Use masked/synthetic production data; cover normal, boundary (exactly 90 DPD, at limit), and exception cases (duplicates, nulls, currency mismatches). Validate data BEFORE UAT — "the UAT data itself is wrong" is the #1 day-one failure.

Defect Lifecycle & Severity/Priority

  1. Log (repro steps, expected vs actual, screenshot) → Triage (defect review board) → Assign → Fix → Retest → Close.
  • Severity = impact; Priority = urgency. S1 Critical (system down / wrong regulatory report — blocks go-live), S2 Major (core function fails, no workaround), S3 Minor, S4 Cosmetic.
  • JIRA standard; track open-vs-closed trend, defect leakage (prod defects that should've been caught in UAT).
  • Weekly defect report: total logged, open by severity, fixed, verified, waivered, blockers to sign-off; ageing >5 days.

Sign-off Criteria & Coordination

  • No open S1/S2 (or formal waivers); all Must-haves tested & passed; 100% scenario execution; known-issues list with workarounds.
  • Daily UAT stand-up (10 min); defect review board (twice weekly); single UAT tracker; translate dev jargon ↔ business language.

How do you handle UAT when the client keeps finding bugs?
Don't react defensively. (1) Triage — defect or new requirement? (2) Confirm data/environment before blaming code (many "bugs" are test-data issues). (3) Get an agreed expected result per failing scenario — if the tester can't state it, the requirement is ambiguous. (4) Prioritize severity×priority; protect go-live by trading Should-haves. (5) Daily defect review + show a declining bug-trend so they see convergence.

22. Deployment & Release Management

Environments

DEV → SIT → UAT → PROD (+ PREPROD, DR). Config alignment matters — "it works in UAT but not prod" is usually an environment/config difference.

Strategies

  • Big bang — replace everything at once; high risk; for tightly coupled cores.
  • Phased/rolling — by branch/country/product (roll EWS to UAE first, then Saudi); lower risk, dual-running.
  • Blue-green — two identical prod environments; instant rollback.
  • Canary — small slice of users, then ramp; rare in core banking.

Go-Live / Cutover / Rollback / Hypercare

  • Cutover plan: minute-by-minute steps with named owners: freeze → backups → stop interfaces → data migration → deploy → configure → smoke test → open to users.
  • Data migration: reconcile counts/totals/hash totals (records in vs out), handle duplicates/dirty data before go-live, validate lookups, keep old system until proven.
  • Rollback vs roll-forward: restore vs fix-forward. For data migrations, restore may be impossible after new transactions — plan "fall back to old system + re-enter the window's transactions", or phase the release.
  • CAB: every prod change needs a Change Request reviewed by the Change Advisory Board (risk level, impact, outage window, rollback plan).
  • Hypercare: 2–6 weeks war-room, dedicated queue, faster SLA, known-issues register; exit when incident rate drops to BAU.
  • Go/No-Go: objective criteria — UAT sign-off, S1/S2 resolved, data reconciled, rollback rehearsed, training done. No-go is legitimate.
  • PIR (4–8 weeks after): business case met? defect leakage, performance vs NFR, lessons learned.
Data consultant's moment: post-deploy smoke tests + reconciliation checks (totals before vs after cutover) are your job. Baseline reconciliations (counts, totals, hash totals, lookups) defined pre- and post-cutover.

23. Banking-Specific IT Constraints

  • Privacy & residency: GDPR, Saudi PDPL, UAE Federal PDPL 2021, Singapore PDPA, India DPDP Act; data residency in-country or approved zones; cloud needs regulator approval (CBUAE guidance).
  • PCI-DSS: card data encryption, segmentation, quarterly scans, restricted storage.
  • Encryption: AES-256 at rest, TLS 1.2+ in transit, masking in non-prod, tokenization for card numbers, HSM key management.
  • Regulatory deadlines: CBUAE daily liquidity, monthly/quarterly prudential returns; MAS timelines; BCBS 239 = legal mandate for data lineage/accuracy/completeness/timeliness. ETL must hit hard deadlines with zero tolerance.
  • Audit readiness: every change documented, signed, traceable; evidence retained per policy.
  • IT general controls / SOX-like: access reviews, change-management evidence, segregation of duties, approval workflows, audit logs.
  • Access: RBAC, least privilege, quarterly recertification, segregation of duties (approver ≠ booker), 2FA, PII access logging.
Answer framing: "I designed the dashboard so credit analysts see only their business unit's data — that's least-privilege and segregation-of-duties, which also satisfies access recertification."

25. Trends 2024–2026

AML

  • AI/ML mainstream — anomaly detection + risk scoring; regulators (HKMA 2024–26, EBA, FATF) encourage adoption but require model risk management and explainability (XAI) — audit trails showing WHY a decision was made. Hybrid stacks: rules + ML + graph analytics.
  • Real-time monitoring — instant payments (UAE IPP, SG FAST, HK FPS) demand near-real-time screening; "instant payments demand instant AML".
  • Perpetual KYC (pKYC) — continuous event-driven refresh; reduces stale-risk and review backlogs.
  • Regtech/suptech — HKMA digitalised AML/CFT supervision; automated SAR narratives.
  • Crypto/VASP AML — Travel Rule (FATF R16; ISO 20022 alignment June 2025), VASP licensing (HK 2023, EU MiCA), stablecoin regulation (HK 2024), on-chain analytics; ~84% of illicit VA volume in stablecoins.
  • Enforcement shift — regulators penalise ineffective systems (stale rules, poor tuning, FP floods), not just absent programs — "TM adequacy enforcement"; KPI-focused exams.

Credit Risk & EWS

  • Real-time decisioning — rule engines + AI scoring + API decisioning; >90% STP in retail/SMB; "always-on" monitoring replaces monthly batch.
  • Alternative data — bank-transaction/cash-flow analysis, open banking (GCC/India account aggregators), rent/telco for thin-file borrowers.
  • ML credit models — gradient boosting/ensembles; ESG/climate into credit; explainable AI (SHAP, reason codes) + drift monitoring; EU AI Act + model-risk expectations.
  • IFRS 9 automation — end-to-end ECL engines (staging + PD/LGD/EAD + macro scenarios + attribution); SICR trigger libraries; month-end close cut 40–60%; RBI ECL from 1 Apr 2027; climate-aware ECL; PMAs/overlays under scrutiny (BoE SS1/23).
  • Gen-AI — early-warning summaries, credit-memo drafting, document extraction, unstructured news → watchlist scoring; adoption > pilots but constrained by data quality, explainability, and hallucination risk. "Workflow design matters as much as model quality."
  • EWS evolution (EY 2025) — from backward-looking to real-time predictive EWS; three pillars: data, analytics/technology, process/governance.
